I Built This Website to Escape AI Slop. I Used AI to Do It.

By Brendon James Kirk August 9, 2025 6 min read

Let's be honest: the internet feels like it's collapsing.

Our digital lives are dominated by two toxic forces. First, the walled gardens of social media, engineered to addict us to an endless scroll of outrage and envy. Second, and more recently, the rising tide of "AI slop"—a grey goo of meaningless, machine-generated articles, images, and comments burying any hope of authentic discovery. It's a world of noise, devoid of signal.

I wanted out. I needed a quiet corner of the web to call my own, a space for deliberate thought, free from algorithmic manipulation and generative sludge.

So I decided to build this website. And in a moment of what I can only describe as profound irony, I built this refuge from the machine with the machine. It was all conceptually born from a single prompt.

This wasn't a surrender; it was a subversion. I call the method "Vibe Coding."

Fighting Fire With Fire: Vibe Coding as an Act of Intent

Let me be clear: I love AI. I'm in awe of its power and the incredible, positive use cases that are emerging every day. Its ability to act as a creative partner, a tireless assistant, and a problem-solving engine is genuinely transformative. That's precisely why the flood of "AI slop" feels so tragic. It's a profound misuse of revolutionary technology.

The problem with AI-generated content isn't the AI itself. The problem is the lack of human intention behind it. "Slop" is born from lazy, soulless prompts: "write me 10 articles about productivity," "generate an image of a happy family." The goal is volume, not value. The result is a digital world that feels increasingly synthetic and empty.

Vibe Coding is the antidote. It's a method of using AI that is rooted in deep, human-centric intention. You don't ask the AI to think for you; you command it to build for you, based on a blueprint that is uniquely yours.

One is automation. The other is directed creation.

The Manifesto That Became My Website

My prompt wasn't just a set of technical specifications; it was a manifesto for the kind of space I wanted to exist in. It was a protest, encoded. Here is the essence of that prompt:

The Philosophy: This website is a protest against the modern internet. It is a quiet room in a loud city. Every design choice must serve calm, focus, and deliberate thought. Reject all trends. The user's focus is sacred.

The Aesthetic: Brutalism meets monasticism. Think concrete, shadow, and stark typography. A monochrome palette with human warmth. Imagine a single, perfectly printed book in an otherwise empty art gallery.

The Feeling: A visitor should feel their shoulders drop when they arrive. Their heart rate should slow. It is a place for reading, not scrolling.

The AI's first pass was stunning. It didn't just understand the instructions; it understood the intent.

Of course, it wasn't a single command that built the final product. While the project was born from that one manifesto, bringing it to life was a different story. It was a three-week dialogue. Using a mix of Gemini for high-level concepts and structure, and GitHub Copilot for the line-by-line implementation, I went through eleven distinct versions of this site.

It was a process of refinement, of pushing the AI to adhere more strictly to the human-centric philosophy I had laid out. "The space between the lines feels cramped. Give the words more room to breathe," I'd command. "Preserve the sense of calm on mobile view. Let the elements stack with dignity."

The result is what you see now. A small piece of the web built not to capture my attention, but to earn it.

This Isn't Magic, It's Prompt Engineering

Which leads me to my next topic. The back-and-forth process I just described—the dialogue, the refinement, the countless attempts to get the feeling right—is the very essence of a critical skill: prompt engineering.

"Vibe Coding" is my name for a philosophy of prompt engineering, one that prioritizes human intent and aesthetic feeling over simple task completion. It's about moving from being a mere user of AI to being its director. It requires patience, clarity, and a strong vision for what you want to create. Getting a machine to understand a "vibe" is an art form in itself, and it's a craft I'm still learning.

We Are Not Helpless

The world may feel like it's on the edge of collapse, but we are not passive victims of the tools we create. This experiment proved to me that the problem isn't the technology; it's our intent.

We can use these powerful tools to churn out more noise, or we can use them with precision and soul to build things that are beautiful, functional, and deeply human. We can build shelters. We can create signal.

The choice is still ours. My question to you is: What refuge will you build?

Thanks for reading. — Brendon
